What estate planning involves

Estate planning is simply putting your wishes in writing, in documents the law will honor, so the people you love are not left guessing.

I knew in law school that this is where I wanted to spend my career, and the reason is simple. Having your affairs in order is a gift in both directions. It gives you the calm of knowing it is settled. It gives your beneficiaries, your heirs, and everyone you leave behind a clear map instead of a pile of questions.

Here is the part most people do not know. If you have no documents, New York State law decides who receives what. Not you. The state applies a fixed formula, and that formula has no idea who took care of you, who you promised something to, or what you actually wanted.

Complete, properly drafted paperwork takes the guesswork out of your final wishes. It cuts down on delay, indecision, and the kind of family arguments that start over how assets get divided and never really end. I also advise on protecting assets from creditors, and on protecting them from the state or county if you or someone you love ever needs state assistance.

The goal is a documented plan, written down and ready, so the transfer of what you own is smooth and seamless for the people who come next.

What real estate work involves

Almost everyone buys or sells some interest in real estate at some point. It is usually the largest transaction of a person’s life, and it should not feel like a leap of faith.

There is also a direct line between this work and estate work. Very often, when someone passes, there is real property that has to be sold or transferred, and the family is handling that in the middle of an already hard season. That connection is exactly why these two areas go hand in hand for me. When one attorney handles both the estate and the sale of the house, nothing falls in the gap between two offices. You explain your situation once.

I have represented hundreds of buyers and sellers, both as part of estate administration and in ordinary purchases and sales.

I also handle deed transfers, where ownership of a house, condo, or co-op is transferred to family members, or into a trust to protect the asset. These are short documents with long consequences, and they are worth doing correctly the first time.

Straight answers

Common Questions

What is the difference between probate and administration?

They are two different court processes, and which one applies depends on one thing: whether there is a will.

If your loved one left a valid will, the process is called probate. The court reviews the will, confirms it is genuine, and appoints the executor named in it to carry out the instructions.

If there is no will, the process is called administration. The court appoints someone, usually a close family member, to serve as administrator. That person distributes everything according to a formula set by New York law, because there are no written instructions to follow.

Both processes run through the same court and both involve paperwork, deadlines, and notice to family members. Probate generally goes more smoothly, because the wishes are already written down.

What is Surrogate’s Court?

Surrogate’s Court is the New York court that handles estates. Every county has one, including each of the five boroughs, Nassau, and Suffolk.

It is where a will is filed and approved, where an executor or administrator is officially appointed, and where disputes over an estate get resolved. It is a quieter, more paperwork-driven court than most people picture. In the great majority of estates nobody ends up arguing in front of a judge at all.

The case is filed in the county where your loved one lived, and I handle the filings and the correspondence so the family is not chasing forms.

What happens if someone passes away in New York without a will?

The estate is distributed under New York’s intestacy rules. Intestacy simply means dying without a valid will, and when that happens the state provides the instructions instead of the person.

The formula is rigid. Depending on who survives, it splits things among a spouse, children, parents, or more distant relatives in a set order. It does not account for a caregiver who gave up years of their life, a promise made at a kitchen table, a stepchild who was never legally adopted, or a family member you were estranged from.

It also means someone has to petition the court to be appointed administrator before anything can be handled at all, which adds time and cost. This is the single strongest argument for getting documents in place while it is easy to do.

Why do I need an attorney for a home closing in New York?

Because New York is an attorney state. In many parts of the country a title company or an escrow agent runs the closing. Here, attorneys represent both the buyer and the seller, and the contract of sale itself is drafted and negotiated by counsel.

On the buyer side, I review the contract, examine the title report, handle the lender’s requirements, and sit at the closing table with you. On the seller side, I draft the contract, clear up any title issues, and make sure you walk away with clean proceeds and no loose ends.

If you have bought a house in another state and remember a much lighter process, this is why the experience here is different.

What does transferring a deed into a trust actually do?

A deed is the document that proves who holds title to a property. Transferring it into a trust means the trust becomes the owner of record, while you keep the benefit of the property under the terms you set up.

Two practical effects. First, property held in a trust generally passes to the people you named without going through Surrogate’s Court, which saves your family time and expense. Second, depending on how the trust is written, it can provide a layer of protection for the asset, including in situations where you or a spouse may later need state assistance.

I want to be careful here. A trust is not a magic shield, the protection depends entirely on the type of trust and on timing rules that matter a great deal. Some transfers need to happen well in advance to do what people hope they will do. That is a conversation worth having early, and I am happy to walk you through what would apply to your situation.
